\begin{abstract}

Proof of useful intelligence (PoUI) replaces hash puzzles with useful AI computation. But, a consensus rule must still score validators, verify the claimed computation, and fit its cryptographic messages within a fix block. Three PoUI bottlenecks are modeled: post quantum payload size, proof soundness, and validator scoring. The scoring mechanism is implemented by the simulator, but a blockchain is not used. We establish a four term trust score and demonstrate score boundaries, an expected score difference between real and fake reports, a one shot incentive result, and a geometric exclusion time result under an explicit independent audit model. All 15 injected malicious nodes are eliminated in each of ten seeds in a 100 node Monte Carlo with sampling accuracy and latency. The mean exclusion epoch is $3.12$, which is near the model prediction of $1/0.35\approx2.86$. We present a challenge proof score protocol flow, validate an EZKL proof pipeline on a small affine ReLU model(6.91\,s prove, 27.5\,ms verify on our computer), identify five circuit needs, and estimate ML-DSA byte sizes using FIPS 204. The outcome is a replicable feasibility analysis and implementation needs, rather than performance data for a consensus protocol.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Blockchain consensus, ML-DSA, ML-KEM, ZKML, underconstrained circuits, trust score, and proof of useful intelligence
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Proof of work in the Bitcoin method expends energy on a search that is worthless outside of consensus~\cite{nakamoto2008}. Proof of stake substitutes capital for that search~\cite{saleh2021pos}. Several fields of study, such useful proof of work~\cite{lihu2020pouw,baldominos2019coinai}, and more recently proof of useful intelligence (PoUI)~\cite{chong2025poui}. attempt to use the same machines on machine learning tasks instead. Model outputs are already paid for by markets like Bittensor, but instead of using a deterministic verifier, they rely on peer scoring within subnets~\cite{bittensor2024}.

If PoUI is going to be used as a consensus rule, three engineering problems come up at once.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Scoring.} Accuracy, latency, and uptime must be combined into a figure that is difficult to inflate by dividing identities or submitting a convincing looking fake output.
  \item \textbf{Proof soundness.} A SNARK or comparable evidence that "the model ran" is only as reliable as the circuit. In general purpose ZK systems, underconstrained circuits are a known class of bugs~\cite{pailoor2023pldi}, neural network devices (ReLU, ArgMax, quantized softmax) add more ways to cheat.
  \item \textbf{Post quantum bytes.} FIPS 204 signatures are significantly larger than Ed25519~\cite{nistfips204,rfc8032}. Replacing validator signatures without considering block size is not recommended.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Research topic and methodology}
 What verification and message budget requirements must go along with a PoUI trust score, and under what explicit assumptions can it exclude low quality reports? We respond to it with (i) a simple analysis of a score and audit model, (ii) a reproducible Monte Carlo that verifies the model's exclusion time prediction, and (iii) a needs analysis for the post quantum interfaces and ZKML. Instead of being an implementation or a head to head consensus benchmark, the study is purposefully an analysis of requirements and feasibility.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Audited trust score model.} We provide bounds, an expected score gap, a one shot incentive result, and a geometric exclusion time theorem under specific audit and independence assumptions.
  \item \textbf{Model consistency evaluation.} A 100 node and ten seed Monte Carlo analysis produces a typical malicious exclusion epoch of $3.12$, which corresponds to the analytical predictive value of $2.86$.
  \item \textbf{Protocol and implementation requirements.} We define a challenge resistant score flow, combine five essential ZKML constraint verifications, and recognize FIPS 204 byte and cross layer failure mode limitations.
  \item \textbf{ZKML pipeline smoke test.} We create, validate, and confirm a deterministic two input affine ReLU ONNX model using EZKL. The provided artifacts and script showcase the complete toolchain, rather than serving as a benchmark for a useful model.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Evaluation boundary}
The simulator does not perform training on or execute a neural model, facilitate network message exchanges, or assess PQC operations. While we incorporate a small EZKL proof smoke test, we do not assert that this constitutes an EZKL/Halo2 audit, general ZKML performance, a repeated game Nash equilibrium, local PQC benchmarks, or that it outperforms an existing proof of stake protocol. The voting baseline of the simulator is merely a 2\% per epoch simplistic process designed to demonstrate the contrast between slashing triggered by automatic audits and governance removal that occurs after a delay.

NIST standardized ML-KEM and ML-DSA in 2024~\cite{nistfips203,nistfips204}.  Baseri et al.\ evaluated the risk of ledger migration during quantum attacks~\cite{baseri2025quantum}.These studies size signatures for everyday transactions. They do not budget for a proving-intensive PoUI slot.
This work connects these strands at the requirement level.
The score indicates what should be measured, the circuit determines what can be trusted, and the PQC payload must fit in to the protocol's timing and byte budgets.

\section{Threat Model and Scope}
\label{sec:threat}

A permissionless validator set of size $N$ is used. Each epoch $t$ a node provides an inference accuracy proxy $A_t\in[0,1]$, a latency $L_t\geq 0$ (ms), an uptime ratio $U_t\in(0,1]$, and a slash bit $S_t\in\{0,1\}$. Honest nodes draw $A_t$ from a clipped Gaussian and $L_t$ from a truncated Gaussian (Section~\ref{sec:sim}). Malicious nodes draw $A_t$ uniformly from $[0.10,0.30]$ and audited with probability $p_{\mathrm{detect}}=0.35$ whenever $A_t$ is below a threshold $\tau=0.40$. Honest nodes are never slashed in this model (false positive rate zero). The final option is described as a limitation and is optimistic.

The investigation focuses on low quality fabricators whose reported accuracy falls below the audit level. Duplicating an identity does not improve its anticipated score. It does not provide Sybil resistance to identity formation without cost, collusion, adaptive reporting, network level eclipse attacks, stake grinding, or associated latency.

The simulator presently refers to ZKML as a verification tool rather than a callable feature. Section~\ref{sec:zkml}'s taxonomy serves as a list of requirements for a future circuit rather than a measurement of an assembled graph.

Key and signature sizes are based on FIPS 204 Table~2~\cite{nistfips204}. % TODO(authors): "the spreadsheet" is never introduced and no timings appear in this paper. Cite the repo file or delete this sentence.
The spreadsheet uses microsecond figures as placeholders for clarity. These are neither SUPERCOP or liboqs measurements from this group.

\section{Trust Score}
\label{sec:trust}

\begin{definition}[Trust score]
For epoch $t\in\mathbb{N}$,
\begin{equation}
  T_t = \alpha A_t^2 e^{-\lambda L_t} + \beta \ln U_t - \gamma S_t,
  \label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
with weights $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\lambda>0$. Experiments use $\alpha=0.5$, $\beta=0.3$, $\gamma=1.5$, $\lambda=0.01$. A node leaves the active set when $T_t < \tau_{\mathrm{excl}}=-0.5$. Implementations should floor $U_t$ at $\epsilon_U>0$ so that $\ln U_t$ is finite; the simulator uses $\max(U_t,10^{-9})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Independence and audits]
\label{ass:ind}
$A_t$ and $L_t$ are independent. Nearly all honest nodes have $S_t=0$. A forged report with $A_t<\tau$ is slashed independently with probability $p\in(0,1]$; if $A_t\ge\tau$ then $S_t=0$. (The simulator assumes $\tau=0.40$ and $p=0.35$.)
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Bounds]
\label{thm:bounds}
If $A_t\in[0,1]$, $L_t\ge 0$, $U_t\in[\epsilon_U,1]$, and $S_t\in\{0,1\}$, then
\[
  \beta\ln\epsilon_U-\gamma \le T_t \le \alpha.
\]
The upper bound is attained at $(A_t,L_t,U_t,S_t)=(1,0,1,0)$. If the protocol enforces $L_t\ge L_{\min}>0$, the bound tightens to $T_t\le\alpha e^{-\lambda L_{\min}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $A_t^2\le 1$ and $e^{-\lambda L_t}\le 1$, the accuracy term is at most $\alpha$, with equality only if $A_t=1$ and $L_t=0$. Because $\ln U_t\le\ln 1=0$, the uptime term is at most $0$, with equality only if $U_t=1$. Because $S_t\ge 0$, $-\gamma S_t\le 0$, with equality only if $S_t=0$. Adding these three inequalities yields $T_t\le\alpha$. For the lower bound, the accuracy term is $\ge 0$, $\ln U_t\ge\ln\epsilon_U$, and $-\gamma S_t\ge-\gamma$. If $L_t\ge L_{\min}$, then $e^{-\lambda L_t}\le e^{-\lambda L_{\min}}$, so the first term is at most $\alpha e^{-\lambda L_{\min}}$.
\end{proof}

Write $\phi_L:=\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda L_t}]$. Independence in Assumption~\ref{ass:ind} gives $\mathbb{E}[A_t^2 e^{-\lambda L_t}]=\mathbb{E}[A_t^2]\,\phi_L$. We do \emph{not} replace $\phi_L$ by $e^{-\lambda\mathbb{E}[L_t]}$ (Jensen would go the wrong way for the convex map $x\mapsto e^{-\lambda x}$ on $\lambda>0$).

\begin{theorem}[Expected score gap]
\label{thm:sybil}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:ind}, set $U_t=1$ for both kinds and apply the latency law that defines $\phi_L$. Assume an honest node has accuracy $A_H$ and a fabricator reports a (potentially random) $A_F$ with $A_F<\tau$ a.s.\ and $\mathbb{E}[S_F]=p$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Delta := \mathbb{E}[T_H]-\mathbb{E}[T_F]
  = \alpha\phi_L\bigl(\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]-\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]\bigr)+\gamma p.
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
In particular $\Delta>0$ whenever $\gamma p>\alpha\phi_L\bigl(\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]-\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]\bigr)_+$, which holds automatically if $\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]\ge\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\mathbb{E}[T_H]=\alpha\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]\phi_L$ because $S_H=0$ and $\ln 1=0$. $\mathbb{E}[T_F]=\alpha\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]\phi_L-\gamma p$. Subtract.
\end{proof}

Splitting into $k$ identities doesn't increase the per identity expectation in~\eqref{eq:gap}. Score weighted selection therefore does not reward Sybil copies of a low $T$ fabricators. This theorem does not apply to one identity one vote systems with no score or stake/identity cost.

The experimental weights, $\phi_L=\mathbb{E}[e^{-0.01 L}]$ for $L\sim\mathcal{N}(50,10^2)$ truncated at $1$, $\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]\approx 0.90^2+0.05^2=0.8125$ (unclipped Gaussian), and a constant probe $A_F=0.39$, one gets $\Delta\approx 0.73$. The simulator is easier for the defender: $A_F\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0.10,0.30]$ so $\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]=(0.10^2+0.10\cdot 0.30+0.30^2)/3=0.0433$.

\begin{theorem}[Unique maximizer of the one shot payoff]
\label{thm:game}
Fix $L_t$ and $U_t$, and let $\mathbb{E}[S_t\mid A]=p\cdot\mathbf{1}[A<\tau]$ as in Assumption~\ref{ass:ind}. Define $g(A)=\alpha A^2 e^{-\lambda L_t}+\beta\ln U_t-\gamma p\cdot\mathbf{1}[A<\tau]$ for $A\in[0,1]$. If $\tau\in(0,1]$ and $p,\alpha,\gamma>0$, then $g$ has a unique maximizer at $A=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c:=\alpha e^{-\lambda L_t}>0$ and $b:=\beta\ln U_t$, so $g(A)=cA^2+b-\gamma p$ on $[0,\tau)$ and $g(A)=cA^2+b$ on $[\tau,1]$. Since $\tau\le 1$, $g(1)=c+b$. For any $A<1$ we have $cA^2<c$, hence $g(A)<c+b-\gamma p\,\mathbf{1}[A<\tau]\le c+b=g(1)$. Every $A<1$ is therefore strictly worse than $A=1$.
\end{proof}

This is a single agent optimization rather than a Nash equilibrium in a repeating game with stakes or collusion. The simulator does not have a channel for measuring Real At on hidden probes, such as ML-KEM-wrapped gold standard inputs.

\begin{lemma}[One slash suffices]
\label{lem:slash}
Assume $U_t=1$, $\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}<0$, and $\gamma>\alpha-\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}$. Then $S_t=0$ implies $T_t\ge 0>\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}$ (using $A_t^2 e^{-\lambda L_t}\ge 0$), while $S_t=1$ implies $T_t\le\alpha-\gamma<\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}$. Thus exclusion occurs if and only if $S_t=1$.
\end{lemma}

The experimental numbers, $\alpha-\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}=1.0$ and $\gamma=1.5>1.0$, so the lemma applies.

\begin{theorem}[Geometric exclusion time]
\label{thm:geom}
Assuming a malicious node has $A_t<\tau$ every epoch,  each epoch represents a separate slash trial with success probability $p$. Let $M$ be the first epoch, and $S_t=1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:slash}, state that $M$ is the exclusion epoch, $M\sim\mathrm{Geometric}(p)$ on $\{1,2,\ldots\}$, $\mathbb{E}[M]=1/p$, and
\[
  \Pr(M\le k)=1-(1-p)^k.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assumption~\ref{ass:ind} states that trials are independent, hence $P(M>k)=(1-p)^k$. Lemma~\ref{lem:slash} identifies $\{M\le k\}$ and excludes epoch $k$.
\end{proof}

For $p=0.35$, $\mathbb{E}[M]\approx 2.86$ and $\Pr(M\le 8)=1-0.65^8\approx 0.968$. Section~\ref{sec:sim} includes Monte Carlo analysis alongside this prediction.

\section{Protocol Sketch}
\label{sec:protocol}

The score is only meaningful if $A_t$ is linked to a specific challenge and model. During registration, validators record stake, ML-DSA verification key, ML-KEM encryption key, and commitment to the authorized model/version. For epoch $t$, a coordinator generates a nonce from the previously finalized block, encrypts a hidden labeled probe with the validator's ML-KEM key, and commits to $(t,\mathrm{id},\mathrm{nonce},\mathrm{model})$. The validator returns $(y,\pi)$, signs $H(t\parallel\mathrm{model}\parallel\mathrm{commitment}\parallel y\parallel H(\pi))$, and submits it by the deadline. Verifiers evaluate the epoch binding, signature, model commitment, circuit proof, and hidden label score before updating $T_t$.

This flow prevents simple replay and model substitution only if the circuit binds the challenge, model, quantization parameters, and output. It does not describe leader election, finality, dispute resolution, or an incentive compatible stake mechanisms, with must be provided by the underlying consensus protocol. The full message level specification and deployment boundary are in the companion \texttt{protocol\_spec.md}.

\section{Simulation}
\label{sec:sim}

\paragraph{Setup}
$N=100$ nodes (70 honest, 15 high latency, and 15 malicious). The horizon spans 1{,}000 epochs. We evaluated the fixed seed 42 and the ten seed set provided below: \texttt{simulation/run\_multiseed.py} replicates that aggregate. The accuracy is $\mathcal{N}(0.90,0.05^2)$ clipped to $[0,1]$, the latency is $\mathcal{N}(50,10^2)$\,ms, leveled at 1\,ms, and high latency nodes add $3\times10$\,ms. Malicious accuracy is $\mathrm{Unif}[0.10,0.30]$. Slash probability is $0.35$ for malicious nodes with $A_t<0.40$. Uptime stays near 1 because nodes are not presented as crashing, so the $\ln U_t$ term is almost inactive.

\paragraph{PoUI outcome}
On seed 42, all 15 malicious nodes were excluded (mean epoch $2.53$, min $1$, max $8$), whereas no honest node was excluded. Repeating the sample process on ten seeds $\{42,7,13,99,123,256,512,1024,2024,31415\}$ consistently removed $15/15$ malicious nodes every time and $0$ honest nodes. The mean of the ten per seed exclusion means was $3.12$ (sample standard deviation $0.71$), compared to the analytical prediction $\mathbb{E}[M]=1/0.35\approx2.86$. A two sided 95\% $t$ interval for the mean of the 10 per seed is $[2.61,3.63]$, which matches the prediction. The simulation serves as a sanity check for the stated audit model, but dose not provide independent security validation due to its implementation in the data creation process.

\paragraph{Sensitivity} The companion calculation considers audit probability and false positive rate. For $p\in\{0.10,0.20,0.35,0.50,0.75\}$, the estimated exclusion times are $\{10,5,2.86,2,1.33\}$ epochs. With one slash exclusion, a per epoch false positive rate of $10^{-3}$ result in an honest validator probability $1-(1-10^{-3})^{1000}\approx0.632$ for at least one incorrect slash every 1{,}000 epochs. A deployable procedure requires calibrated detection plus repeated evidence, an appeal process, or a less brittle slash rule. The zero false positive simulator is intentionally optimistic.

\paragraph{Baseline}
We also used a stake weighted committee with 10 nodes each epoch. To remove malicious members, a Bernoulli trial with $p=0.02$ is required at each epoch (a toy vote). On seed 42, 13 out of 15 malicious nodes (86.7\%)  were successfully deleted, with an average epoch of $568$. Automatic slash is faster than the straw procedure. This is not a comparison to Ethereum or other live proof of stake protocols.

\paragraph{What was not simulated}
The simulation did not involve training or evaluating any neural networks. There were no packets delivered, and latency is a scalar draw, not a network topology.

\section{ZKML Circuit Checklist}
\label{sec:zkml}

If a later implementation utilizes a circuit $\mathcal{C}$ for $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$, soundness in usually defined as
\[
  \mathrm{Verify}(\mathcal{C},x,y,\pi)=1 \implies y=f(x).
\]
Pailoor et al.\  found that many genuine circuits allow for extra witnesses~\cite{pailoor2023pldi}. When using a PoUI verifier, it's crucial to ensure that the given score aligns with the model, input, and inference semantics, rather than simply having a proof. The following five conditions address the question of common inference gadgets. The design requirements are based on literature and circuit semantics, we did not compile EZKL circuits or generate exploit traces. 

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_zkml_taxonomy.jpg}
  \caption{Five circuit soundness requirements for a PoUI inference verifier.}
  \label{fig:zkml_taxonomy}
\end{figure}

\textbf{V-I Range.} An explicit range proof $w\in[0,2^k-1]$ (or a similar lookup) is required for ReLU, sigmoid, and $k$-bit quantized wires. Without it, a prover can use out of range activations to fulfill the polynomial equations.

\textbf{V-II Witness choice.} ArgMax, TopK, and tied softmax scores can admit more than one legal index. To force a total order, use the formula \ $\ell[i^*]\ge\ell[j]+\epsilon$ for all $j\neq i^*$.

\textbf{V-III Layer skip.} If only the I/O relation is limited, intermediate activations can be created. Require $h_{k+1}=f_k(h_k)$ per layer (or similar residual identity).

\textbf{V-IV Quantization slack.} Instead of being left as provider-selected slack that traverses logits over a decision boundary, scale $s$ and zero point $z$ must be bound in the instance (or in a commitment).

\textbf{V-V Wrong model.} The circuit must open a commitment $H(\theta)$ to the actual weights used. Otherwise, the prover proves another network, $f_{\theta'}$.

These are necessary device level inspections.
To ensure a compiler's soundness, future implementations should verify the backend, quantization technique, and model graph.

\paragraph{EZKL smoke test}
To ensure the suggested proof interface is executable, we created a deterministic ONNX model $\mathrm{Gemm}(2\times2)\to\mathrm{ReLU}$, generated a witness, then produced and verified a proof with EZKL 23.0.5. On an Intel Core i3 1005G1 laptop, proving took 6.91\,seconds, verification 27.5\,milliseconds, and the proof JSON size was 17{,}790 bytes. The test uses a locally produced SRS and a small model, making it a toolchain smoke test rather than a security or performance benchmark. The objective is to show that IC-01/IC-02 (Section~\ref{sec:codesign}) may be instantiated, but it does not validate the five checklist elements for a production compiler or model.

\section{Layer Contracts}
\label{sec:codesign}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} is a requirements diagram, not a running stack. It specifies where future implementations must share data and enforce deadlines.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_architecture.png}
  \caption{Challenge proof score requirements and deployment risks for a future PoUI implementation.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

IC-01 (L1$\to$L2): Request a proof of $y=f_\theta(x)$ under commitment $H(\theta)$, with an example budget of $5$ seconds and around $200$ KB.
Failures include timeout, underconstrained accept, and out of memory.

IC-02 (L2$\to$L1): submit $(y,\pi)$ with an ML-DSA signature on $H(\pi)$. Failures: late slot, poor signature, verifier reject $\Rightarrow S_t=1$.

IC-03 (L1$\to$L3): register an ML-DSA key, bootstrap cannot occur on a classical only channel if harvest now decrypt later is in scope.

IC-04 (L3$\to$L2): verification keys overlap for one epoch, allowing proofs initiated under key $k$ can still be checked after rotation.

EZKL class proving times for small quantized models are measured in seconds rather than sub-seconds~\cite{ezkl2024}. A one second BFT slot cannot simultaneously wait for such a proof. The $5$\,s budget is simply an example, a protocol should be tailored to its own model and hardware. The design implies asynchronous commit     reveal, where a commitment might be proposed in slot $s$ and the evidence opened later. Without the pipeline, slots may skip (FM-CD-1), and without a key overlap window, in flight proofs may fail during rotation (FM-CD-2). A classical only bootstrap can compromise long term confidentiality (FM-CD-3), whereas individual PQC signatures and raw proofs may exceed a block budget (FM-CD-4).


Signing the proof hash with the validator key (ESP-1) results in regular non repudiation: $\mathrm{id}\to H(\pi)\to (x,y)$. This does not necessarily show that $\mathcal{C}$ was sound. Slashing on verifier reject (ESP-3) is only as fair as the circuit.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

N equals $100$, and ten seeds are little. Detection is a coin flip with no false positives, uptime is consistent, and the vote baseline is illustrative, not a proof of stake model. The ZKML smoke test is limited to a single device with a local test SRS and does not cover all EZKL, Halo2, or Groth16 devices. Theorem~\ref{thm:game} is one shot, but Lemma~\ref{lem:slash} requires $U_t=1$. The PQC discussion uses standard byte sizes rather than locally measured cryptographic operations. The research makes an analytical feasibility assertion rather than a deployment performance claim because of these boundaries.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper clarifies the minimum evidence chain required for a PoUI consensus design: an auditable score, a challenge proof score flow, a circuit that binds the claimed output to the prescribed model, and a cryptographic payload that fits the protocol budget. Under the stated independent audit model, the trust score analysis predicts geometric exclusion and the Monte Carlo is consistent with that prediction. A small EZKL proof demonstrates the selected tool chain, while the sensitivity calculation shows why false positive handling cannot be omitted. The next stage prototype must implement hidden probe delivery, a ceremony derived SRS, a nontrivial compiled circuit, and measured PQC operations before it can make end to end security or performance claims.
