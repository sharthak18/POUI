\begin{abstract}

Proof of useful intelligence (PoUI) proposes using AI computation as useful work in a consensus design. A deployable system must define how validators are scored, how claimed computations are verified, and how cryptographic messages fit within a block budget. This paper presents an analytical feasibility study, not a blockchain implementation. We define a four term trust score and derive score bounds, an expected score gap, a one agent score maximization result under stated assumptions, and a geometric exclusion time result under an independent audit model. In a 100 node simulation using sampled accuracy and latency proxies and assuming zero honest false positives, all 15 malicious nodes were excluded in each of ten seeds. The mean of the per seed mean exclusion epochs was $3.12$ (standard deviation $0.71$), compared with the model prediction $1/0.35\approx2.86$. We sketch a challenge/proof/score flow, list five ZKML circuit review questions, and report FIPS/RFC parameter sizes and illustrative payload arithmetic. A tiny EZKL proof pipeline run is a smoke test, not a production audit or benchmark. No consensus network or local PQC primitive benchmark is implemented.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Blockchain consensus, ML-DSA, ML-KEM, ZKML, underconstrained circuits, trust score, and proof of useful intelligence
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Proof of work in the Bitcoin method expends energy on a search that is worthless outside of consensus~\cite{nakamoto2008}. Proof of stake substitutes capital for that search~\cite{saleh2021pos}. Several fields of study, such useful proof of work~\cite{lihu2020pouw,baldominos2019coinai}, and more recently proof of useful intelligence (PoUI)~\cite{chong2025poui}. attempt to use the same machines on machine learning tasks instead. Model outputs are already paid for by markets like Bittensor, but instead of using a deterministic verifier, they rely on peer scoring within subnets~\cite{bittensor2024}.

If PoUI is going to be used as a consensus rule, three engineering problems come up at once.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Scoring.} Accuracy, latency, and uptime must be combined into a score. This study does not model identity splitting or demonstrate resistance to false outputs that evade a real audit.
  \item \textbf{Proof soundness.} A SNARK or comparable evidence that "the model ran" is only as reliable as the circuit. In general purpose ZK systems, underconstrained circuits are a known class of bugs~\cite{pailoor2023pldi}, neural network devices (ReLU, ArgMax, quantized softmax) add more ways to cheat.
  \item \textbf{Post quantum bytes.} FIPS 204 signatures are significantly larger than Ed25519~\cite{nistfips204,rfc8032}. Replacing validator signatures without considering block size is not recommended.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Research topic and methodology}
 What verification and message budget requirements must accompany a PoUI trust score, and under what explicit assumptions can it exclude low quality reports? We address this question with (i) an analysis of a score and audit model, (ii) a reproducible Monte Carlo that compares sampled outcomes with the model's exclusion time prediction, and (iii) a review of post quantum interfaces and ZKML design considerations. This is an analysis of requirements and feasibility, not an implementation or a head to head consensus benchmark.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Analytical trust score model.} We provide bounds, an expected score gap, a one agent score maximization result, and a geometric exclusion time theorem under specific audit and independence assumptions. The maximization result does not establish truthful reporting.
  \item \textbf{Model consistency check.} Across ten seeds, the mean of the per seed mean exclusion epochs is $3.12$ (standard deviation $0.71$), compared with the model prediction of $2.86$. This is a sanity check, not independent security validation.
 \item \textbf{Protocol and implementation considerations.} We sketch a challenge/proof/score flow, list five ZKML circuit review questions, and discuss FIPS 204 parameter sizes and cross layer design risks.
  \item \textbf{ZKML pipeline smoke test.} We create, validate, and confirm a deterministic two input affine ReLU ONNX model using EZKL. The provided artifacts and script showcase the complete toolchain, rather than serving as a benchmark for a useful model.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Evaluation boundary}
The simulator does not train or execute a neural model, exchange network messages, or measure PQC operations. The small EZKL run is a toolchain smoke test, not an EZKL/Halo2 audit or general performance result. The vote baseline is a toy rule: its $0.02$ exclusion probability applies only after a malicious validator is selected for a 10 member committee. It is not representative of proof of stake protocols.
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Chong et al.\ present PoUI as an effective integration of AI work with staking~\cite{chong2025poui}. We incorporate a clear four term score along with an exclusion threshold, providing proofs solely under the audit model outlined in Section~\ref{sec:trust}. Lihu et al.\ and Baldominos and Saez incorporate training or inference within the mining loop~\cite{lihu2020pouw,baldominos2019coinai}. DeepChain examines collaborative learning that is compatible with incentives and features on chain audit trails~\cite{weng2021deepchain}. Bittensor is the most similar deployed market. It's trust relies on social and subnet specific factors~\cite{bittensor2024}.
Groth's SNARK and PLONK backends are commonly used in proofs~\cite{groth2016,plonk2019}. Pailoor et al.\ (PLDI 2023) developed an automated method for identifying underconstrained circuits~\cite{pailoor2023pldi}. Peng et al.\ conducted a survey on verifiable machine learning, including cost and quantification~\cite{peng2025zkml}. EZKL builds ONNX style graphs into circuits~\cite{ezkl2024}. Worldcoin employs zero knowledge proofs for biometric uniqueness, a distinct threat model from validator inference~\cite{worldcoin2023}.

NIST standardized ML-KEM and ML-DSA in 2024~\cite{nistfips203,nistfips204}.  Baseri et al.\ evaluated the risk of ledger migration during quantum attacks~\cite{baseri2025quantum}.These studies size signatures for everyday transactions. They do not budget for a proving intensive PoUI slot.
This work connects these strands at the requirement level.
The score indicates what should be measured, the circuit determines what can be trusted, and the PQC payload must fit in to the protocol's timing and byte budgets.

\section{Threat Model and Scope}
\label{sec:threat}

The model considers a validator set of size $N$. Each epoch $t$, a node has an inference accuracy proxy $A_t\in[0,1]$, latency $L_t\geq0$ (ms), uptime ratio $U_t\in(0,1]$, and slash bit $S_t\in\{0,1\}$. Honest accuracy is drawn from a Gaussian clipped to $[0,1]$, latency is drawn from a normal distribution floored at $1$ ms. High latency nodes receive an additional $30$ ms. Malicious accuracy is drawn uniformly from $[0.10,0.30]$, when $A_t<\tau=0.40$, a node is independently slashed with probability $p_{\mathrm{detect}}=0.35$. Honest nodes are never slashed in this model, this zero false positive assumption is optimistic.

The model focuses on low quality fabricators whose reported accuracy falls below the audit threshold. It does not model identity creation costs, identity splitting, or stake weighted selection and therefore does not establish Sybil resistance.

The simulator presently refers to ZKML as a verification tool rather than a callable feature. Section~\ref{sec:zkml}'s taxonomy serves as a list of requirements for a future circuit rather than a measurement of an assembled graph.

Key and signature sizes are reported from the cited standards. The repository script calculates parameter sizes and illustrative payloads, it does not measure cryptographic operation timings.

\section{Trust Score}
\label{sec:trust}

\begin{definition}[Trust score]
For epoch $t\in\mathbb{N}$,
\begin{equation}
  T_t = \alpha A_t^2 e^{-\lambda L_t} + \beta \ln U_t - \gamma S_t,
  \label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
with weights $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\lambda>0$. Experiments use $\alpha=0.5$, $\beta=0.3$, $\gamma=1.5$, $\lambda=0.01$. A node leaves the active set when $T_t < \tau_{\mathrm{excl}}=-0.5$. Implementations should floor $U_t$ at $\epsilon_U>0$ so that $\ln U_t$ is finite, the simulator uses $\max(U_t,10^{-9})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Independence and audits]
\label{ass:ind}
$A_t$ and $L_t$ are independent. Nearly all honest nodes have $S_t=0$. A forged report with $A_t<\tau$ is slashed independently with probability $p\in(0,1]$, if $A_t\ge\tau$ then $S_t=0$. (The simulator assumes $\tau=0.40$ and $p=0.35$.)
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Bounds]
\label{thm:bounds}
If $A_t\in[0,1]$, $L_t\ge 0$, $U_t\in[\epsilon_U,1]$, and $S_t\in\{0,1\}$, then
\[
  \beta\ln\epsilon_U-\gamma \le T_t \le \alpha.
\]
The upper bound is attained at $(A_t,L_t,U_t,S_t)=(1,0,1,0)$. If the protocol enforces $L_t\ge L_{\min}>0$, the bound tightens to $T_t\le\alpha e^{-\lambda L_{\min}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $A_t^2\le 1$ and $e^{-\lambda L_t}\le 1$, the accuracy term is at most $\alpha$, with equality only if $A_t=1$ and $L_t=0$. Because $\ln U_t\le\ln 1=0$, the uptime term is at most $0$, with equality only if $U_t=1$. Because $S_t\ge 0$, $-\gamma S_t\le 0$, with equality only if $S_t=0$. Adding these three inequalities yields $T_t\le\alpha$. For the lower bound, the accuracy term is $\ge 0$, $\ln U_t\ge\ln\epsilon_U$, and $-\gamma S_t\ge-\gamma$. If $L_t\ge L_{\min}$, then $e^{-\lambda L_t}\le e^{-\lambda L_{\min}}$, so the first term is at most $\alpha e^{-\lambda L_{\min}}$.
\end{proof}

Write $\phi_L:=\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda L_t}]$. Independence in Assumption~\ref{ass:ind} gives $\mathbb{E}[A_t^2 e^{-\lambda L_t}]=\mathbb{E}[A_t^2]\,\phi_L$. We do \emph{not} replace $\phi_L$ by $e^{-\lambda\mathbb{E}[L_t]}$ (Jensen would go the wrong way for the convex map $x\mapsto e^{-\lambda x}$ on $\lambda>0$).

\begin{theorem}[Expected score gap]
\label{thm:score gap}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:ind}, set $U_t=1$ for both kinds and apply the latency law that defines $\phi_L$. Assume an honest node has accuracy $A_H$ and a fabricator reports a (potentially random) $A_F$ with $A_F<\tau$ a.s.\ and $\mathbb{E}[S_F]=p$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Delta := \mathbb{E}[T_H]-\mathbb{E}[T_F]
  = \alpha\phi_L\bigl(\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]-\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]\bigr)+\gamma p.
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
In particular $\Delta>0$ whenever $\gamma p>\alpha\phi_L\bigl(\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]-\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]\bigr)_+$, which holds automatically if $\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]\ge\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\mathbb{E}[T_H]=\alpha\mathbb{E}[A_H^2]\phi_L$ because $S_H=0$ and $\ln 1=0$. $\mathbb{E}[T_F]=\alpha\mathbb{E}[A_F^2]\phi_L-\gamma p$. Subtract.
\end{proof}

The expected score gap compares individual score distributions under the assumptions above. It does not model identity multiplication, stake allocation, or validator selection and therefore does not establish Sybil resistance.

The formal analysis script uses $\phi_L=\mathbb{E}[e^{-0.01 L}]$ for latency drawn from a normal distribution with mean $50$ ms and standard deviation $10$ ms, floored at $1$ ms. Under the script's clipped honest accuracy distribution and the simulator's $A_F\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0.10,0.30]$ fabricator distribution, the model derived expected score gap is approximately $0.75917$. This is an analytical result under the stated assumptions, not a measured performance result.

\begin{theorem}[Unique maximizer of the one shot payoff]
\label{thm:game}
Fix $L_t$ and $U_t$, and let $\mathbb{E}[S_t\mid A]=p\cdot\mathbf{1}[A<\tau]$ as in Assumption~\ref{ass:ind}. Define $g(A)=\alpha A^2 e^{-\lambda L_t}+\beta\ln U_t-\gamma p\cdot\mathbf{1}[A<\tau]$ for $A\in[0,1]$. If $\tau\in(0,1]$ and $p,\alpha,\gamma>0$, then $g$ has a unique maximizer at $A=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c:=\alpha e^{-\lambda L_t}>0$ and $b:=\beta\ln U_t$, so $g(A)=cA^2+b-\gamma p$ on $[0,\tau)$ and $g(A)=cA^2+b$ on $[\tau,1]$. Since $\tau\le 1$, $g(1)=c+b$. For any $A<1$ we have $cA^2<c$, hence $g(A)<c+b-\gamma p\,\mathbf{1}[A<\tau]\le c+b=g(1)$. Every $A<1$ is therefore strictly worse than $A=1$.
\end{proof}

This is a single agent optimization, not a Nash equilibrium in a repeated game with stakes or collusion. The simulator does not compare a reported accuracy with hidden ground truth, so the maximizer does not establish truthful reporting.

\begin{lemma}[One slash suffices]
\label{lem:slash}
Assume $U_t=1$, $\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}<0$, and $\gamma>\alpha-\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}$. Then $S_t=0$ implies $T_t\ge 0>\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}$ (using $A_t^2 e^{-\lambda L_t}\ge 0$), while $S_t=1$ implies $T_t\le\alpha-\gamma<\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}$. Thus exclusion occurs if and only if $S_t=1$.
\end{lemma}

The experimental numbers, $\alpha-\tau_{\mathrm{excl}}=1.0$ and $\gamma=1.5>1.0$, so the lemma applies.

\begin{theorem}[Geometric exclusion time]
\label{thm:geom}
Assume a malicious node has $A_t<\tau$ in every epoch and that each epoch has an independent slash event with probability $p$. Define $M=\min\{t\geq1:S_t=1\}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:slash}, $M$ is the exclusion epoch and $M\sim\mathrm{Geometric}(p)$ on $\{1,2,\ldots\}$, with $\mathbb{E}[M]=1/p$ and
\[
  \Pr(M\le k)=1-(1-p)^k.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assumption~\ref{ass:ind} makes the slash trials independent, so $\Pr(M>k)=(1-p)^k$. Therefore $\Pr(M\leq k)=1-(1-p)^k$.
\end{proof}

For $p=0.35$, $\mathbb{E}[M]\approx 2.86$ and $\Pr(M\le 8)=1-0.65^8\approx 0.968$. Section~\ref{sec:sim} includes Monte Carlo analysis alongside this prediction.

\section{Protocol Sketch}
\label{sec:protocol}

The score is only meaningful if $A_t$ is linked to a specific challenge and model. During registration, validators record stake, ML-DSA verification key, ML-KEM encryption key, and commitment to the authorized model/version. For epoch $t$, a coordinator generates a nonce from the previously finalized block, encrypts a hidden labeled probe with the validator's ML-KEM key, and commits to $(t,\mathrm{id},\mathrm{nonce},\mathrm{model})$. The validator returns $(y,\pi)$, signs $H(t\parallel\mathrm{model}\parallel\mathrm{commitment}\parallel y\parallel H(\pi))$, and submits it by the deadline. Verifiers evaluate the epoch binding, signature, model commitment, circuit proof, and hidden label score before updating $T_t$.

This flow prevents simple replay and model substitution only if the circuit binds the challenge, model, quantization parameters, and output. It does not describe leader election, finality, dispute resolution, or an incentive compatible stake mechanisms, with must be provided by the underlying consensus protocol. The full message level specification and deployment boundary are in the companion \texttt{protocol\_spec.md}.

\section{Simulation}
\label{sec:sim}

\paragraph{Setup}
$N=100$ nodes (70 honest, 15 high latency, and 15 malicious) are simulated for 1{,}000 epochs. We evaluated seed 42 and the ten seed set listed below, \texttt{simulation/run\_multiseed.py} reproduces the aggregate. Honest accuracy is $\mathcal{N}(0.90,0.05^2)$ clipped to $[0,1]$. Latency is $\mathcal{N}(50,10^2)$ ms, floored at 1 ms, high latency nodes receive an additional 30 ms. Malicious accuracy is $\mathrm{Unif}[0.10,0.30]$. The slash probability is $0.35$ when a malicious node has $A_t<0.40$. Uptime is set near 1 because crashes are not modeled, so the $\ln U_t$ term is almost inactive.

\paragraph{PoUI outcome}
On seed 42, all 15 malicious nodes were excluded (mean epoch $2.53$, min $1$, max $8$), whereas no honest node was excluded. Repeating the sample process on ten seeds $\{42,7,13,99,123,256,512,1024,2024,31415\}$ consistently removed $15/15$ malicious nodes every time and $0$ honest nodes. The mean of the ten per seed exclusion means was $3.12$ (sample standard deviation $0.71$), compared to the analytical prediction $\mathbb{E}[M]=1/0.35\approx2.86$. A two sided 95\% $t$ interval for the mean of the 10 per seed is $[2.61,3.63]$, which matches the prediction. The simulation serves as a sanity check for the stated audit model, but dose not provide independent security validation due to its implementation in the data creation process.

\paragraph{Sensitivity} The companion calculation considers audit probabilities and false positives. For $p\in\{0.10,0.20,0.35,0.50,0.75\}$, the expected geometric exclusion times are $\{10,5,2.86,2,1.33\}$ epochs. Under independent trials, a per epoch false positive rate of $10^{-3}$ gives an individual honest validator probability of $1-(1-10^{-3})^{1000}\approx0.632$ of at least one false slash over 1{,}000 epochs. This is a per validator calculation, not a system wide probability. A deployable procedure requires calibrated detection, evidence retention, and an appeal process. The simulator's zero false positive assumption is intentionally optimistic.

\paragraph{Baseline}
The selected member vote is a toy rule with a 10 member committee: a malicious validator has a $0.02$ exclusion probability only after being selected. On seed 42, 15 of 15 malicious validators were excluded, the mean exclusion epoch was $405.8$ and the median was $410$ among excluded validators. This is a single run toy result, not a representative proof of stake benchmark, no general speedup ratio is calculated.

\paragraph{What was not simulated}
The simulation did not involve training or evaluating any neural networks. There were no packets delivered, and latency is a scalar draw, not a network topology.

\section{ZKML Circuit Checklist}
\label{sec:zkml}

If a later implementation utilizes a circuit $\mathcal{C}$ for $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$, soundness in usually defined as
\[
  \mathrm{Verify}(\mathcal{C},x,y,\pi)=1 \implies y=f(x).
\]
Pailoor et al.\ found that some genuine circuits admit unintended witnesses~\cite{pailoor2023pldi}. A PoUI verifier must bind its score to the intended model, input, and inference semantics, having a proof alone is insufficient. The following five items are design review questions, not universal necessary or sufficient conditions. No EZKL circuit was compiled or exploit trace generated for this checklist. 

\begin{figure}[!b]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_zkml_taxonomy.jpg}
  \caption{Five design review questions for a future PoUI inference circuit, not a circuit audit or universal soundness requirements.}
  \label{fig:zkml_taxonomy}
\end{figure}

\textbf{V-I Range.} For bounded or quantized values, check that the circuit's field to integer representation and range match the intended model. Use range constraints or lookups where required by the encoding, whether a missing constraint affects soundness depends on the actual circuit.

\textbf{V-II Witness choice.} ArgMax and TopK outputs need defined tie semantics. Specify whether ties permit multiple valid outputs or require a deterministic tie break, then constrain the circuit to those semantics, an arbitrary strict margin rule may change the intended function.

\textbf{V-III Layer skip.} If only the I/O relation is limited, intermediate activations can be created. Require $h_{k+1}=f_k(h_k)$ per layer (or similar residual identity).

\textbf{V-IV Quantization semantics.} Bind the scale, zero point, rounding, signedness, clipping, and overflow rules to the authorized model/configuration and ensure the circuit implements those rules.

\textbf{V-V Model binding.} If weights are supplied dynamically, bind them to the authorized model/version, for example through a checked commitment. If weights are fixed in the circuit, bind the circuit/version identifier to the protocol's authorized model.

These are possible review checks, which are needed depends on the model, encoding, and proof system. A future audit should inspect the compiled constraints, backend, quantization semantics, and model graph.

\paragraph{EZKL smoke test}
To ensure the suggested proof interface is executable, we created a deterministic ONNX model $\mathrm{Gemm}(2\times2)\to\mathrm{ReLU}$, generated a witness, then produced and verified a proof with EZKL 23.0.5. On an Intel Core i3 1005G1 laptop, proving took 6.91\,seconds, verification 27.5\,milliseconds, and the proof JSON size was 17{,}790 bytes. The test uses a locally produced SRS and a small model, making it a toolchain smoke test rather than a security or performance benchmark. The objective is to show that IC-01/IC-02 (Section~\ref{sec:codesign}) may be instantiated, but it does not validate the five checklist elements for a production compiler or model.

\section{Layer Contracts}
\label{sec:codesign}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} is a requirements diagram, not a running stack. It specifies where future implementations must share data and enforce deadlines.

\begin{figure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_architecture.png}
  \caption{Proposed challenge/proof/score interfaces and unresolved design scenarios, not an implemented stack.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

IC-01 (L1$\to$L2): Request a proof of $y=f_\theta(x)$ under commitment $H(\theta)$, with an example budget of $5$ seconds and around $200$ KB.
Failures include timeout, underconstrained accept, and out of memory.

IC-02 (L2$\to$L1): submit $(y,\pi)$ with an ML-DSA signature over a domain separated digest binding the epoch, validator identity, challenge commitment, model/configuration version, output hash, and proof hash. Verification rejection alone does not establish malicious intent or imply a slash, penalties require a separately specified evidence and appeal policy.

IC-03 (L1$\to$L3): authenticate the registration and bind ML-DSA and optional ML-KEM keys to the validator identity. ML-KEM does not authenticate a public key by itself. HNDL is a confidentiality risk for classical encryption, not for a signature channel.

IC-04 (L3$\to$L2): verification keys overlap for one epoch, allowing proofs initiated under key $k$ can still be checked after rotation.

EZKL class proving times for small quantized models are measured in seconds rather than sub-seconds~\cite{ezkl2024}. A one second BFT slot cannot simultaneously wait for such a proof. The $5$\,s budget is simply an example, a protocol should be tailored to its own model and hardware. The design implies asynchronous commit     reveal, where a commitment might be proposed in slot $s$ and the evidence opened later. Without the pipeline, slots may skip (FM-CD-1), and without a key overlap window, in flight proofs may fail during rotation (FM-CD-2). A classical only bootstrap can compromise long term confidentiality (FM-CD-3), whereas individual PQC signatures and raw proofs may exceed a block budget (FM-CD-4).


Signing a context bound submission digest attributes those message bytes to the registered key, subject to the key management assumptions. A signature does not establish that $\mathcal{C}$ is sound or that the inference is correct. A verifier rejection alone is not sufficient evidence for slashing, the protocol needs defined evidence, false positive handling, and appeal rules.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

N equals $100$, and ten seeds are little. Detection is a coin flip with no false positives, uptime is consistent, and the vote baseline is illustrative, not a proof of stake model. The ZKML smoke test is limited to a single device with a local test SRS and does not cover all EZKL, Halo2, or Groth16 devices. Theorem~\ref{thm:game} is one shot, but Lemma~\ref{lem:slash} requires $U_t=1$. The PQC discussion uses standard byte sizes rather than locally measured cryptographic operations. The research makes an analytical feasibility assertion rather than a deployment performance claim because of these boundaries.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper outlines candidate evidence requirements for a future PoUI consensus design: a defined score, a challenge/proof/score flow, a circuit that binds outputs to the prescribed model, and a specified message budget. Under the stated independent audit model, the analysis predicts geometric exclusion and the Monte Carlo results are consistent with that prediction. A tiny EZKL run is a toolchain smoke test, while the sensitivity calculation illustrates why false positive handling matters. A future prototype would need to implement and evaluate hidden probe delivery, use an appropriately trusted setup or setup independent proof system, audit a nontrivial compiled circuit, and measure PQC operations before making end to end security or performance claims.
